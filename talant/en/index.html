Logline

Hands that play, and hands that strike

To buy back an antique dombra pawned by his alcoholic father, and to perform in the final of the TV music competition Talent, a young dombra virtuoso is forced into the cage of brutal underground bare-knuckle fights.

The drive and nerve of Whiplash, the brutality of The Raid, the family drama of Warrior. A story about fathers and sons, about the price of talent and the right to be heard.

For producers

This story is set in Almaty

  • The script is not tied to a city

    A TV studio, a pawnshop, an old garage, an underground fight club, a teacher’s apartment. Across 121 pages there is not a single Moscow street. Only the character names would change, and nothing needs rewriting.

  • Our hero

    Alex is of mixed Kazakh and Russian descent. A boy from a residential district who plays Kurmangazy’s kuis on the dombra and hits like a champion. He is the face of the young generation of Kazakhstan and all of Central Asia, a generation big cinema has yet to tell its story.

  • An international project

    A universal story of father and son, music and fighting. Export-level fight scenes and sound, sales on world markets and at festivals. The film is being built as a Kazakhstani project with an international destiny.

  • Two companies, one team

    Taurus Asia Production handles live shooting, funding and distribution in Kazakhstan. Neyra Vision handles previs, the AI block and post-production with NEYRA technology. The creative team is assembled and the screenplay is ready.

We break the stereotype of the “other.” Alex is talent, intellect and strength in one person. This is not simply a boxing film but a manifesto of a new generation, shot in Kazakhstan.

Synopsis

Two gifts. One dombra.

Eighteen-year-old Alex has two gifts: he plays the dombra with genius, and he can knock out any opponent.

Five years ago he quit sport after his father, a former coach, traded his son’s world championship for alcohol. Alex finds salvation in music under the wing of his wise mentor, Yan Borisovich, who gives him a priceless dombra made by a great master in 1720 and passed from teacher to pupil.

Alex dazzles his way through the qualifying rounds of the hit TV show Talent, winning over the audience and the stern judge, pop star Anna Prazdnik. His main rival is Anton, a technically flawless virtuoso and the son of a media magnate who is trying to prove his right to music to his father.

Alex’s world collapses when his drunken father pawns the dombra. To buy back the instrument and play his teacher’s dombra in the final, Alex makes a desperate decision.

Through the cynical Anton, who sees a way to profit from his rival, Alex enters a brutal underground bare-knuckle tournament under the name “Talent.” The winner’s prize is exactly 60 million tenge, the same amount it costs to buy back the dombra.

A clash of worlds: the refinement of music and television glamour collide with the blood of underground arenas. Two fronts, intercut, the stage and the cage, converge in a single finale.

Tone, audience, music

Who it’s for, and how it sounds

Tone and style

The drive and psychological tension of Whiplash, the uncompromising brutality of The Raid, the family drama of Warrior.

Target audience

Men and women aged 16 to 60. Worlds that seem incompatible, ancient dombra music and bare-knuckle fighting, combined with sports action, a romantic storyline and family drama, unite several audience segments.

For men and women

Men are drawn in by spectacular bouts, sporting adrenaline and the hero’s fight for his dream. Women by the romance, emotional depth, family ties and the hero’s dramatic fate.

Musical code

Cover versions of songs by Viktor Tsoi and the band Kino, “Kukushka (The Cuckoo),” “Peremen (Change)” and “A Sad Song,” in modern arrangements with a rap collaboration. Songs that people in Kazakhstan know by heart.
